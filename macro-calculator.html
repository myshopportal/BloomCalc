What are macros?

Macronutrients ("macros") are the three main nutrients that give you energy:

  • Protein (4 calories per gram): helps build and repair muscle. Found in eggs, beans, lentils, dairy, fish, chicken and tofu.
  • Carbohydrates (4 calories per gram): your body's main fuel. Choose whole grains, fruit, vegetables and beans.
  • Fat (9 calories per gram): needed for hormones and vitamins. Choose nuts, seeds, olive oil and avocado.

For reference, US dietary guidelines suggest adults get about 10–35% of calories from protein, 45–65% from carbs and 20–35% from fat. "Lower carb" sits below that carb range, so it's a choice to make with care.

This is an estimate, not medical advice. It's meant for healthy adults and isn't designed for pregnancy, breastfeeding, athletes in heavy training, or anyone with a medical condition. For personal advice, talk to your doctor or a registered dietitian.

FAQ

What is a good macro split?

A balanced split like 25% protein, 50% carbs and 25% fat fits most healthy adults and sits within US dietary guideline ranges.

How many grams of protein do I need?

Many adults need roughly 0.8 g of protein per kg of body weight as a minimum. Active people often eat more. See the protein number in your results.

Do I have to hit my macros exactly?

No. Treat them as a rough guide. Getting close most days, with plenty of whole foods, matters more than exact numbers.
